Method

Two kinds of record

Milestones are narrow, dated statements, each tied to an outside source. Passages are sentences from the manuscript of Mind Meets Machine. A passage shows what the author wrote. The manuscript does not corroborate itself, so no passage counts as a checked fact.

Three kinds of connection

Documented relationship: three are asserted, Logo as a design inspiration for Scratch, the 2001 revision of Bloom’s taxonomy, and the 2015 UNESCO recommendation superseding the 1976 text. Editorial link: a milestone and a passage mention the same name, or a curator paired a milestone with a chapter. Neither implies influence. Narrative order: the sequence of a thread or chapter.

What a date means

Each milestone states what its year marks: a preprint, a publication, an opening, a release. Passages carry no date. Narrative tense and the year the book appeared are not used to place them, and scenes with the author’s granddaughter are kept as attributed narrative.

Labels on passages

Anecdote, interpretation, historical assertion, technical claim, proposal, and forecast are labels assigned automatically to help you filter. A passage can carry several, and a forecast stays a forecast until dated outcome evidence is set beside it.

The book

Every passage in this exhibition comes from one book, and the chapter structure is the book’s own.
