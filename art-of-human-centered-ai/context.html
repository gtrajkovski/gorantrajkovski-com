The Art of Human-Centered Context Engineering

The prompt was never the point. What matters is what the model can see when it answers — your actual documents, the memory of what you said, the five facts it needs instead of the five hundred it doesn't.

What it's about

Briefing a colleague.

Picture the best handoff you've ever received at work. Instead of dumping two hundred files on your desk, someone sat down for twenty minutes and told you what actually mattered. You walked away able to do the job — not because you were brilliant, but because someone had done the work of deciding what you needed to know.

A model, like a new colleague, is only as good as the brief it gets. The first book treated that brief as a single message — the prompt. This one is about everything the message sits inside: the documents you make available, the history of what's been said, the facts you pull in and the ones you hold back. That whole surrounding situation is the context, and engineering it well is the difference between a colleague set up to succeed and one handed a folder and a shrug.

The framework

FRAME

Three jobs that make up the work of context engineering — running at once, pulling against each other. The judgment to balance them is the whole craft.

S

Source — what you bring in

Putting the right real material in front of the model at the moment it answers — and trusting where it came from, so answers are grounded instead of guessed.

S

Select — what you leave out

The discipline of omission. A bad inclusion is visible; a bad omission leaves no trace. Less, ordered well, beats more.

S

Sustain — what persists over time

Memory, state, and the relational thread — what carries from one exchange to the next, done humanely, with the right to inspect and forget.

“Every context is an act of editing, and editing is never neutral. What I put in front of a model shapes what it can conclude.” From the manifesto
