Follow a story

Short journeys through connected records. Stops are chosen to illustrate a question, not to prove a conclusion; "next in this story" is narrative order, not historical influence.

The future of learning, according to the past In development

What people predicted, when and why, what the evidence says happened, and what remains open. No comparison here is complete: each still needs paired, dated outcome evidence, and a card may be published with no verdict if that evidence proves inadequate. Claims are paraphrased.

Method

Review workflow

Written, then source-checked (each claim matched to a located passage, each link opened), then editorially reviewed, then published. A correction route will open at public launch.

Three kinds of connection

Documented relationship: a historical link that needs evidence. Scholarly comparison: a parallel drawn for understanding, with no influence implied. Editorial link: a journey sequence or shared theme. Closeness on the coil implies nothing.

What a date means

Each record states what its date marks: enactment, founding, publication, a surviving artifact, a documented practice, or adoption. Ranges and traditions are shown as spans, never as invented single years.

Time compression

The coil and the timeline give more room to recent centuries, where records are dense. Each change of spacing is labeled on the scrubber. The list view sorts strictly by date.

Audit

Coverage matrix

Records by topic and region. Coverage reflects this collection, not the historical importance or extent of learning in each region. "Gap" means no record; "thin" means one or two, and a populated cell may hold a single example.
